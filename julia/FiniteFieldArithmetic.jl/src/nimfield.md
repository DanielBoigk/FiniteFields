#implementation #characteristic-2 #categorical

> The canonical fields $\mathbb{F}_{2^n} \subset \mathbf{On}_2$ for every $n \le 128$ (backlog J10,
> ADR-0005): `nim_field(n)` is `Nimber` for $n = 2^k$ and `Nim{n, T}` otherwise, an element stored
> as its rank in ordinal order. Products and inverses go through an internal polynomial basis
> (J1a's Barrett arithmetic, J3's Itoh–Tsujii); inclusions, equality and hashing across all
> degrees are those of $\mathbf{On}_2$.

## The type

`Nim{N, T}` for $N \le 128$ not a power of two, `T` the smallest unsigned type with $N$ bits.
`Nim{N, T}(k)` is the element of rank $k$. For $N = 2^k$ the field is an initial segment of the
ordinals and rank = value, so `nim_field(2^k)` returns the existing `Nimber` type: there is one
type per field, as for `PolyElement`. Addition is XOR (the rank is linear). Printing is hex, with
the field named, as for every `BinaryFieldElement` that does not fill its storage.

## Arithmetic

R1 gives, per $N$: an internal generator $g$ (the first rank-basis element of degree $N$), its
minimal polynomial $P_N$, and the ranks of $1, g, \dots, g^{N-1}$. These define two byte-table
linear maps (constants of `@generated` methods, built at first use of a field):
rank coordinates $\to$ coordinates in the basis $g^i$, and back. A product is
`to(a) * to(b)` in `PolyElement{N, T, P_N}` (carry-less product and Barrett reduction), then
`from`. `inv` converts once, inverts there (Itoh–Tsujii, the polynomial-basis default since J3),
and converts back. The choice of $g$ is internal: no rank depends on it.

## Between fields

- `embed(nim_field(n), x)` for $x$ in a field of degree $m \mid n$ (a `Nim` or a `Nimber`): the
  inclusion of $\mathbf{On}_2$, a byte-table linear map from the R1 inclusion matrices.
- `==` across fields: the same ordinal, i.e. the same smallest field and the same rank there;
  `hash` hashes that, and agrees with `Nimber`'s hash when the smallest field is a nimber field.
  So `nim_field(3)(1) == nim_field(43)(1) == GF2(1)`, and `Set`/`Dict` work across degrees.
- Mixed arithmetic promotes to `nim_field(lcm(m, n))` when $\operatorname{lcm}(m, n) \le 128$
  (and fails otherwise): the same rule as for nimber levels, extended to every degree.
- `minimal_degree(x)` (by squaring in the internal basis until $x^{2^d} = x$ with $d \mid N$) and
  `reduce_field(x)` (type-unstable by design, as for nimbers): J9b for the default
  representation. Conway fields keep their own (unimplemented) J9b.

## Tests

`test/nimfield.jl`: types for all $n \le 128$; field axioms, inverses, $x^{2^n} = x$ on every
degree; exhaustive inverses and a cyclic multiplicative group for small $n$; inclusions are
homomorphisms and compose; $\mathbb{F}_8 \subset \mathbb{F}_{64}$ as computed by hand in
$\mathbf{On}_2$; $\mathbb{F}_4$ = ranks 0 … 3 in every even degree; equality, hashing and promotion
across fields; `minimal_degree` against the Frobenius; **agreement with the R1 data** (powers of
$g$ and all inclusion matrices) when `research/` is present; printing; `@inferred`; JET.

Reading:

- A product costs the Conway product plus three byte-table maps (two to the internal basis, one
  back): 1–5 ns more below 32 bits, about 15 ns at 33–64 bits and 35–47 ns above 64 bits (16 table
  lookups of 16 bytes per map). Up to degree 64 that is about nimber GF(2^64) (17 ns); above, about
  1.2–1.5 times nimber GF(2^128). Inverses convert only once, so they are 6–36% above Conway.
- Bulk work could stay in the internal basis and convert at the boundary (as `CLMulMatMul` does for
  nimbers): an expectation, not implemented; the J8 bulk operations are nimber-only so far.
- `minimal_degree` squares up to $N$ times and `hash` calls it (and `reduce_field`): fine for
  small $N$, 1.7–1.8 µs at $N = 126$. A Frobenius-power test per maximal divisor (byte tables of
  $x \mapsto x^{2^d}$) would bring it to a few table maps: an expectation, not implemented.
